Taichung and Taipei with a Baby: Our 8-Day Itinerary

Thirteen of us, three generations, one infant, and a lot of night markets.

In May 2026 we took our first big trip with our baby: eight days in Taiwan, travelling with extended family. Thirteen people in total, from grandparents to a baby under one. The trick was a plan with fixed meeting points and a "nap break" option every day, so the three of us could slip back to the hotel without holding everyone up.

Getting around

  • We flew Singapore to Taoyuan and went straight to Taichung, then moved to Taipei on day 4 by private hire van (about 2.5 hours without traffic). With luggage, a stroller and older relatives, the van was worth it.
  • In both cities, short taxi rides of 6 to 20 minutes covered most hops. In Taipei, the MRT red line is easy for Beitou.

Taichung (days 1 to 4)

Day 1: Arrive, Yizhong Street

Hotel near Yizhong Street, so dinner was a five-minute walk. The fried chicken cutlet there is a must.

Day 2: Second Public Market and Lukang

Breakfast snacks at the Second Public Market (cinnamon rolls, old-style rice balls, mushroom thick soup). Then a half day in Lukang Old Street by bus: the narrow Moru Lane, Tianhou Temple and the Osmanthus Lane art village. Back to Yizhong night market in the evening.

Day 3: Bubble tea, ice cream and Fengjia

Chun Shui Tang's original branch, where bubble tea was invented, then ice cream at Miyahara. Here we split: the three of us went back for the baby's nap, everyone else visited the Lecheng Temple. We met up again at Fengjia Night Market in the evening.

Day 4: Taichung to Taipei

A late brunch of fried pork-chop rice at Fan Ji, then the van to Taipei and an evening on Yongkang Street.

Taipei (days 5 to 8)

Day 5: Shifen and Jiufen

Sky lanterns at Shifen Old Street in the morning, Jiufen in the afternoon, Raohe Night Market at night.

Day 6: Wanhua

Longshan Temple, then a walking food crawl nearby: Tainan-style wan gao, Hong Kong-style milk tea and French toast at Long Du Bing Shi, and fried ba-wan. Finish at the Starbucks inside a restored century-old house.

Day 7: Beitou

Early queue at Fu Hang soy milk (the grandparents volunteered), then the red line to Xinbeitou for Thermal Valley, the Hot Spring Museum and the Beitou library.

What worked with a baby

  • Every day had a fixed meeting point and an optional "family of three" detour for nap time.
  • Mornings were for the big outing. Afternoons were flexible.
  • Night markets were close to the hotel, so going back early was easy.
