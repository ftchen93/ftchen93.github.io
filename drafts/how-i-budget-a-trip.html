How I Budget a Trip (and Where the Money Actually Goes)

A plan before, a tracker during, and a reconciliation after.

I track every trip in a spreadsheet: what I planned to spend, what I actually spent, and why they're different. It started as a habit and turned into the most useful part of planning.

The results so far

TripPlanResult
Japan, 2023Set a budgetAbout 7% under, despite heavy shopping
Taiwan, 2026Tracked dailyOn target, but with a cash gap (see below)
China, 2025No budget setTracked after the fact only

The trips where I didn't set a budget first are the ones I learned least from.

The method

  1. Before: flights and hotels go in as soon as they're booked, since they're usually half the cost.
  2. Cash: log every currency exchange with its rate, so cash spending converts back accurately.
  3. During: a quick daily line for cash spent and card spent.
  4. After: count the leftover cash and reconcile.

The cash gap

On the Taiwan trip, card spending matched perfectly. Cash didn't: of all the cash I exchanged, almost a third never showed up in my log or in my wallet afterwards. Small purchases at night markets, mostly. Card spending tracks itself. Cash needs a habit, like logging at the end of each day.

Splitting costs for a group

For a 13-person family trip, I booked flights and hotels for everyone and collected each household's share into one separate account. That kept the trip money apart from our own. When we changed the hotel plan and the total dropped, the spreadsheet made it easy to work out each household's refund.

  • Split per room and per person, not just per household.
  • Record each payment with its date.
  • Note who covered what for whom (we split the grandparents' share among the siblings).
